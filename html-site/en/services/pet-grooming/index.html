Pet Grooming

At any other groomer, your pet just gets a haircut. Here, they also get a set of veterinary eyes on them. Our in-clinic grooming team flags any skin, ear, or parasite concerns directly to the vet on duty — and if something needs attention, an appointment can be arranged the same day. One visit, two layers of care.

Grooming is not only about appearance. Matted fur pulls on the skin and causes dermatitis; overgrown nails change the way a dog walks; uncleaned ears eventually become infected. Regular grooming stops all three before they start.

What happens in a grooming session

Each session starts with an assessment of the coat, and any matting is worked out first. Then comes a warm bath, a shampoo suited to the coat type, and controlled drying — a damp undercoat is one of the most common causes of skin problems.

Next come breed-specific trimming and styling, nail clipping, ear cleaning, and tidying around the paws. With senior, anxious, or first-time patients we work at the animal's pace — a session forced to completion makes the next one far harder.

The difference grooming inside a clinic makes

Our team sees the whole of the skin, inside the ears, the nail beds, and between the pads — the areas owners rarely get a proper look at. Flea dirt, a tick, redness, scabbing, ear odour, or a lump goes straight to the vet on duty.

Where needed, an examination is arranged the same day, parasite treatment is started, or an appointment is booked for dental care. A small finding never gets the chance to come back months later as a bigger one.

How often grooming is needed

Coat type, season, and where your pet spends its time set the interval. Long-coated breeds typically need a groom every 6–8 weeks, sooner if the coat mats. Short-coated dogs often need no clipping but still benefit from regular bathing, nail trimming, and ear care.

Service Scope

  • Bathing, coat-appropriate shampoo, and controlled drying
  • Breed-specific trimming and styling
  • De-matting and undercoat care

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need an appointment for grooming?

Yes, we recommend booking in advance. Call +90 533 844 91 92 to arrange a convenient time.

How often should I book a grooming appointment?

It depends on breed and coat type. Most dogs benefit from a groom every 6–8 weeks; cats typically need it less often.

Is grooming available for all breeds?

Yes. Our team is experienced in breed-specific trimming and styling, using equipment and shampoo matched to each coat type.

What happens if a health issue is spotted during grooming?

Our team reports any skin, ear, or parasite concern directly to our vet, and an examination can often be arranged the same day.

Do you groom cats as well?

Yes. For cats the focus is de-matting, undercoat care, and nail clipping, with sessions kept short and paced to the cat's stress level.

My pet is frightened of grooming. What do you do?

We work calmly, take breaks, and go at the animal's tolerance, splitting the session across two visits if needed.
