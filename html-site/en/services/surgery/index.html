Surgery

Surgery is never an easy decision — but when it's needed, the right hands make all the difference. Dr. Gökay Yeşilovalı, our small animal surgery specialist, personally leads every procedure. Pre-operative anaesthesia and vital signs are closely monitored; afterwards, a tailored pain management and recovery plan is put in place. We walk you through every step — you won't be left wondering.

How safe an operation is gets decided long before and well after the incision: the right patient assessment, the right anaesthetic protocol, uninterrupted monitoring, and pain genuinely controlled afterwards — all planned individually for every patient.

Preparing for surgery

Your pet is examined head to tail beforehand, and blood tests are requested according to age and general condition. Kidney and liver values determine how anaesthetic drugs are cleared, which makes this the foundation of anaesthetic safety. Where indicated, the site is imaged in advance with X-ray or ultrasound.

Food is withheld for at least 8 hours before surgery; our vet advises separately on water. Once the appointment is confirmed you receive full instructions, including which medications to pause and what time to arrive.

Surgery and recovery

Throughout the procedure, anaesthetic depth, respiration, heart rate, and body temperature are continuously monitored. Once surgery is complete, your pet recovers in a warmed, observed area. For routine neutering, most patients are discharged the same day.

Before you go home we go through the pain relief plan, wound care, use of a collar, and the signs to watch for, and a follow-up for suture removal is scheduled from the outset. If you see redness, discharge, or swelling, call us without waiting.

When is surgery needed?

Beyond neutering, the reasons we see most often are swallowed foreign objects, masses under the skin, wounds and abscesses that won't heal, pyometra in female dogs, urinary blockages, and road accident injuries. We assess the non-surgical options first; surgery is recommended when it genuinely changes the outcome.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best age for neutering?

Generally from 5–6 months of age in dogs and cats. Exact timing depends on breed, sex, and overall health.

Is it safe to neuter my dog or cat?

Yes. It is a routine procedure under safe anaesthesia. Dr. Gökay Yeşilovalı monitors vital signs throughout and prepares a recovery plan for your pet.

How should I prepare my pet for surgery?

Withhold food for at least 8 hours before the operation. Full instructions, including water and current medications, are given when surgery is booked.

Are blood tests necessary before surgery?

We recommend them for every patient receiving an anaesthetic; kidney and liver values directly shape the protocol and reduce the risk.

How long is the recovery period?

Routine neutering usually takes 7–10 days; complex procedures longer. A suture check and follow-up is scheduled for every patient.

Will my pet stay at the clinic after surgery?

For routine procedures, most patients go home the same day once anaesthetic recovery has been observed. After major surgery, observation may be extended.
