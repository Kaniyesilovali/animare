Radiology

Some things can't be seen from the outside. With digital X-ray and ultrasound, we can evaluate bone fractures, organ changes, foreign objects, and pregnancy in the same appointment. All images are digitally archived and can be shared with a specialist when needed. Ultrasound involves no radiation at all — completely safe for your pet.

Limping, repeated vomiting, a swollen abdomen, appetite loss with no obvious cause — each has dozens of possible explanations. Imaging narrows that list fast, so treatment rests on what we can see.

X-ray or ultrasound?

X-ray is the stronger tool for hard tissue: fractures and hairline cracks, hip dysplasia, joint wear, the lung field, bladder stones, and swallowed metal or bone. A single exposure takes seconds, and images are usually taken from two angles.

Ultrasound shows inside soft tissue — liver, kidneys, spleen, intestinal wall, bladder, and uterus, in real time. It picks up organ changes, cysts and masses, free fluid, and pregnancy, and uses no radiation. The two methods complement each other.

What happens during an imaging session?

The procedure is quick: your pet is positioned on the table and the image is taken, rarely more than a few minutes in total. Most patients are scanned without sedation, though light sedation may be suggested for a painful area.

Before an ultrasound a small area of the abdomen is clipped and gel applied — neither step hurts. Digital images appear on screen immediately, are interpreted in the same session, and are saved to your pet's file, ready to read alongside blood results.

When do we recommend imaging?

After a road accident or a fall; for limping lasting more than two days; for repeated vomiting, diarrhoea, or abdominal swelling; for unexplained weight loss or laboured breathing; for cats straining to urinate; and before major surgery. In senior patients we also take baseline images at the annual check-up.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need an appointment for X-ray or ultrasound?

We recommend a general check-up first; our vet decides which imaging is needed. In emergencies, imaging is performed immediately.

Is X-ray radiation harmful to my pet?

Our digital equipment uses a very low dose and exposure lasts only seconds. The diagnostic benefit far outweighs the minimal risk. Ultrasound involves no radiation at all.

How quickly are radiology results available?

Digital images are ready immediately and reviewed during the same appointment. We explain the findings and agree the treatment plan that day.

Does my pet need to be sedated for imaging?

Most pets are scanned without sedation. Light sedation may be suggested for painful conditions such as suspected fractures, or for very anxious patients.

Can you confirm pregnancy with ultrasound?

Yes. Ultrasound confirms pregnancy in dogs and cats from around day 25, and can also monitor litter size and foetal viability.

Is any preparation needed before an ultrasound?

Withhold food for 8–12 hours so intestinal gas doesn't obscure the image; water can be given as normal. We confirm this when you book.
