Laboratory

Guesswork has no place in veterinary care. Our in-clinic laboratory — supervised by Dr. Hazel Tamakan Yeşilovalı, a microbiology specialist — runs complete blood counts, biochemistry panels, urinalysis, parasite screening, and rapid infectious disease tests, with most results back the same day. That means less time between examination and a treatment plan.

An animal cannot describe pain or fatigue. Blood, urine, and faecal samples do it for them, showing in numbers how hard the kidneys are working and whether the immune system is fighting an infection.

Which test, and when?

A complete blood count (CBC) measures red cells, white cells, and platelets — the first sign of anaemia, infection, or clotting problems. A biochemistry panel shows kidney, liver, and pancreatic values with blood glucose and electrolytes, essential before any anaesthetic.

Urinalysis gives quick answers on kidney function, urinary tract infection, and crystal formation; in cats it is key to catching blockage risk early. Faecal screening identifies intestinal parasites, and rapid tests detect FIV, FeLV, parvovirus, and ehrlichia within minutes.

Why same-day results matter

Sending a sample to an outside laboratory means waiting until the next day, sometimes longer — and for a vomiting, lethargic, or feverish patient that delay pushes back treatment. Our tests run on our own analysers, so we often discuss results while you are still in the consulting room.

Results are stored in your pet's file, so values can be compared at the next visit. For chronic kidney disease, diabetes, or thyroid disorders, that history shows at a glance whether treatment is working.

How samples are taken

Blood is usually drawn from a foreleg vein in a few seconds, and most patients tolerate it easily. For urine we either wait for a natural sample or collect a sterile one under ultrasound guidance. A faecal sample can be brought from home, ideally collected that day.

Frequently Asked Questions

How quickly are test results available?

Most in-clinic tests produce results within minutes to a few hours, allowing same-day diagnosis and treatment. Microbiological cultures need longer.

Should my pet be fasting before a blood test?

Fasting for 8–12 hours is recommended for certain biochemistry panels; water can still be given. Our vet will tell you in advance which tests require it.

What conditions can laboratory tests detect?

Kidney and liver disease, anaemia, infections, parasites, diabetes, thyroid disorders, and many infectious diseases can all be identified through laboratory testing.

What laboratory tests are available at Animare?

Complete blood counts, biochemistry panels, electrolytes, urinalysis, parasite (faecal) screening, and rapid infectious disease tests are all run in our own laboratory.

Are blood tests necessary before surgery?

We recommend them for every patient receiving an anaesthetic. Kidney and liver values determine how the drugs are cleared, so screening measurably reduces the risk.

Does a healthy-looking pet still need testing?

For pets over seven, a basic blood and urine screen once a year. Kidney disease in particular shows in the values long before any symptom appears.
