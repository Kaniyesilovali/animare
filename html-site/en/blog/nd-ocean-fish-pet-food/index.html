N&D Ocean: Which Cats and Dogs Suit the Fish Range?

12 October 2026 · Dr. Gökay Yeşilovalı, Veterinary Surgeon

A dog that scratches more after starting a chicken-based food, or a cat that turns its nose up at every new bag. Many owners who come to the clinic arrive with the same question: should we try a fish-based food? N&D Ocean is the Farmina range that takes most of its protein from fish and seafood. This article covers what is in N&D Ocean, which cats and dogs it suits, who it does not suit, and how it differs from the other N&D ranges.

In short

  • In N&D Ocean, protein comes mainly from fish and seafood such as cod, salmon, herring and shrimp.
  • It is one of the first alternatives we try when a chicken protein sensitivity is suspected.
  • The name "Ocean" describes the protein source; check the ingredient list on the bag to see whether it contains grain.
  • There are puppy and adult, kitten and adult, and neutered cat variants, plus wet (tinned) options.
  • Being fish-based does not make it a therapeutic food: kidney disease or a confirmed food allergy needs a veterinary diet.

What Is N&D Ocean?

What usually separates the N&D ranges is the protein source. Prime relies on land animals such as chicken and lamb; Ocean relies on the sea. The Ocean formulas we stock are built on fish proteins such as cod, salmon, herring and shrimp. We compared all the ranges side by side in our Farmina N&D pet food guide.

The name "Ocean" describes the protein source of the range, not whether the food contains grain. If your pet needs to avoid grain, check the ingredient list on the bag, or let us choose the right bag with you at the clinic.

Dry and wet options

On the dog side there are puppy and adult formulas; on the cat side, kitten, adult and neutered formulas. For wet food there are tins for cats with tuna, cod, shrimp and pumpkin (kitten and adult), and a salmon and cod tin for dogs. Everything we keep in stock is listed in our cat and dog food range article.

What Does Fish Protein Add?

Omega-3 fatty acids

Fish is a natural source of omega-3 fatty acids such as EPA and DHA. They support the skin barrier, add shine to the coat and help keep inflammation in check. That is why Ocean often comes up when a pet arrives with dry skin, dandruff or a dull coat.

Food is still not a treatment for skin disease. If fleas, a fungal infection or an ear infection sit behind the itching, changing the food will not fix it on its own. If ear scratching is part of the picture, our article on otitis externa can help.

A different protein source

Most food allergies in cats and dogs come not from grain but from commonly used proteins such as chicken or beef. If your pet has eaten chicken-based food for years, fish may be a protein its immune system has met far less often. That is what makes Ocean one of the first alternatives when chicken sensitivity is suspected.

Taste and acceptance

Many fussy cats respond well to the smell and taste of fish. In cats with a reduced appetite, or those that resist a change of food, the wet version of Ocean makes our job easier.

Which Cats and Dogs Does N&D Ocean Suit?

  • Pets with a suspected chicken protein sensitivity. If itching, ear problems or soft stool seem linked to a chicken-based food, it is one of the first options to try after an examination.
  • Pets with poor skin and coat quality. The omega-3 content supports dry skin and a dull coat.
  • Fussy cats. The fish flavour makes moving to a new food easier.
  • Neutered cats. The variant prepared for this group has a more moderate energy density. For neutered dogs, see our article on N&D Quinoa Neutered.

Who Does It Not Suit?

  • Pets with a fish or seafood allergy. It is uncommon, but an allergy to fish can develop. In that case the whole Ocean range is ruled out.
  • Cats with kidney disease. Chronic kidney disease calls for a renal diet with adjusted phosphorus and protein; Ocean is not a renal food. See our article on chronic kidney disease in cats for details.
  • Pets with a confirmed food allergy. Once an allergy is diagnosed, an elimination diet is run under veterinary supervision with hydrolysed or special diets. A commercial fish-based food does not replace that process.

How to Move Your Pet Onto N&D Ocean

Spread the change over 7-10 days. For the first three days make a quarter of the food Ocean, then half and half for the next three, then three quarters, and switch fully on day ten. Because the protein source changes, stool consistency may shift slightly at first. If diarrhoea lasts more than 48 hours, contains blood, or comes with loss of appetite, stop the transition and contact the clinic.

Set the portion against your pet's ideal weight, not today's weight. Fish-based foods are tasty, so overeating is common; use kitchen scales rather than a cup.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is N&D Ocean grain-free?

The name Ocean describes the protein source of the range, not the grain status. Grain content can differ from bag to bag, so do not decide on the range name alone. If your pet needs to avoid grain, check the ingredient list on the bag, or let us choose with you at the clinic.

Can I feed N&D Ocean to a dog with a chicken allergy?

Ocean is a common alternative for dogs with a suspected chicken sensitivity. If an allergy has been confirmed by examination and an elimination diet, though, the next step is a special diet recommended by your vet rather than a commercial food.

Does fish-based food cause urinary problems in cats?

A balanced fish-based food does not cause urinary problems on its own. What matters most for urinary health is water intake and mineral balance; switching part of the daily portion to wet food is the easiest step. For the warning signs, see our article on urinary blockage in cats.

Is N&D Ocean suitable for kittens and puppies?

Yes, the range has kitten and puppy formulas. Energy and mineral needs during growth differ from those of an adult, so give a growing animal the puppy or kitten formula, not the adult one.

How do I move my pet onto N&D Ocean?

Spread the change over 7-10 days: a quarter new food for the first three days, half and half for the next three, then three quarters, and the full new food by day ten. Because the protein source changes, stool consistency may shift slightly at first. If diarrhoea lasts more than 48 hours, contains blood, or comes with loss of appetite, stop the transition and contact the clinic.
