N&D Tropical: The Low-Grain Range With Tropical Fruits

20 September 2026 · Dr. Gökay Yeşilovalı, Veterinary Surgeon

A bag with banana, mango and papaya on the front raises the same question every time: what do they add, or is it just packaging? N&D Tropical is the range in which Farmina adds five tropical fruits to its ancestral-grain formula — and it is one of the bags we pick up most often during a nutrition consultation as a Nicosia vet practice. This article covers what is in the range, what the fruit really contributes, which cats and dogs it suits, who it does not suit, and where it parts ways with Prime and Ancestral Grain.

In short

  • Tropical is low-grain, not grain-free — it contains whole spelt and whole oats.
  • Five dried tropical fruits are used: banana, kiwi, mango, papaya and pineapple.
  • Most of the protein is animal-based; lamb, chicken and fish options exist.
  • Dogs get mini and medium & maxi kibble, puppy and adult; cats get adult and neutered variants.
  • It does not suit an animal with a confirmed grain sensitivity — that is where the grain-free ranges come in.
  • The fruit blend is part of an everyday food, not a supplement or a treatment.

What Is N&D Tropical?

Among the Farmina N&D ranges, Tropical sits next to Ancestral Grain: the skeleton is the same, so grain is not removed — low glycaemic index ancestral cereals, whole spelt and whole oats, are used instead. The difference is the blend of dried banana, kiwi, mango, papaya and pineapple. Protein still comes mainly from animal sources, with lamb, chicken and fish-based variants. We compared all the ranges side by side in our Farmina N&D pet food guide.

On the dog side there are mini and medium & maxi kibble sizes in puppy and adult versions; on the cat side, a separate option for neutered cats sits alongside the adult formula. Everything we keep in stock is listed in our cat and dog food range article.

What Do the Tropical Fruits Add?

Antioxidants and vitamins

Kiwi is rich in vitamin C, pineapple in vitamin B6 and thiamine; mango and papaya carry carotenoids and polyphenols, compounds that help balance oxidative load at cell level. Be realistic about the scale, though: the proportion of fruit in a dry food is nowhere near enough to cover an animal's daily vitamin needs, and it does not need to be — the formula is balanced separately with a vitamin and mineral premix. The fruit is a bonus, not the backbone.

Fibre and satiety

The dried fruit and beet pulp add some fibre. Combined with the low glycaemic index carbohydrates of spelt and oats, the result is a profile that raises blood sugar more slowly after a meal and keeps a pet full for longer — a practical difference in animals that beg between meals.

Which Cats and Dogs Does Tropical Suit?

  • Healthy adults with no need for grain-free feeding. Grain allergy is relatively uncommon in cats and dogs; for a healthy animal a low-grain formula is a balanced option.
  • Neutered cats. The variant prepared for this group has a higher fibre content and a more moderate energy density.
  • Animals that get hungry between meals or gain weight easily. The low glycaemic carbohydrate and fibre profile helps satiety last.
  • Animals that find high-protein grain-free formulas heavy. With a history of gas or soft stool on Prime, an ancestral-grain range is a reasonable step.

Who Does It Not Suit?

  • Animals with a confirmed grain sensitivity. If grain has to be excluded, the range you want is grain-free Prime.
  • Animals with chronic disease. In kidney, liver or pancreatic disease, or diagnosed obesity, an everyday food is not enough; a veterinary diet is needed.
  • Animals with ongoing diarrhoea. When stool consistency will not settle, the cause comes first. If the plan is to work through the fibre profile, the Pumpkin range is the better fit.
  • Growing puppies and kittens on an adult formula. The range has its own puppy and kitten variants; an adult formula falls short during growth.

Tropical, Ancestral Grain or Prime?

All three are balanced options; the question is not “which is better” but “which variable are we changing”. If grain has to go, Prime. If grain is not the issue and the dog is very active, the Ancestral Grain range stands out with its higher protein level. For a quieter, mostly indoor animal that puts on weight easily, the more moderate energy profile of Tropical makes life easier. The decision comes from the animal rather than the label: age, breed, body condition score, current and target weight, and any medical history.

Portions and the Food Change

The table on the bag is a starting point; read the amount against the target weight, not today's weight. Use kitchen scales rather than a cup, split the daily amount into two meals, and keep treats under 10% of daily calories. Change the food over 7-10 days: a quarter new food for three days, half and half for the next three, then three quarters, and the full new food by day ten. A larger stool volume at first is expected. If a problem lasts more than 48 hours, get in touch with us.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is N&D Tropical grain-free?

No. Tropical is a low-grain range built on whole spelt and whole oats. If you want a grain-free food, the ranges you are looking for are Prime or Pumpkin. What sets Tropical apart is not the removal of grain but the five tropical fruits added on top of the ancestral cereals.

Do the tropical fruits in the food actually do anything?

Banana, kiwi, mango, papaya and pineapple go into the formula dried; they carry vitamin C, polyphenols and a small amount of fibre. The contribution is real but modest: the overall nutritional balance of a food is set by the protein source and the carbohydrate choice, not by the fruit. Do not think of the fruit blend as a treatment or a supplement.

What is the difference between Tropical and Ancestral Grain?

Both are low-grain ranges built on spelt and oats. Ancestral Grain keeps the fruit side to options such as pomegranate and blueberry, while Tropical uses five tropical fruits together and generally comes at a more moderate protein level. For a very active dog Ancestral Grain tends to fit better; for a quieter animal, or one that gains weight easily, Tropical is often the steadier choice.

Can I feed N&D Tropical to a neutered cat?

Yes, the range includes a variant prepared for neutered cats, with a higher fibre content and a more moderate energy density. Even so, what decides weight after neutering is not the range but setting the portion against the cat's ideal weight.

My dog has a grain sensitivity. Does Tropical suit him?

No. If a grain sensitivity has been confirmed on examination, a low-grain range is not appropriate; Prime or Pumpkin would be considered instead. That said, most food allergies come from protein sources such as chicken or beef rather than from grain, so if there is any doubt the animal needs examining before the food is changed.

How do I move my pet onto Tropical?

Spread the change over 7-10 days: a quarter new food for the first three days, half and half for the next three, then three quarters, and the full new food by day ten. Because the fibre and carbohydrate profile changes, you may see a larger stool volume in the first few days; that is expected. If diarrhoea lasts more than 48 hours, contains blood, or comes with loss of appetite, stop the transition and contact the clinic.
