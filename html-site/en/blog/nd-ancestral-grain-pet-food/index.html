What Is N&D Ancestral Grain? The Low-Grain Range

9 September 2026 · Dr. Gökay Yeşilovalı, Veterinary Surgeon

N&D Ancestral Grain is Farmina's low-grain range: instead of removing grain altogether, it uses a limited amount of low-glycaemic “ancestral grains” such as spelt and oats, while the protein still comes mainly from animal sources. The question we hear often at the clinic is this: “Should I go grain-free, or is low-grain enough?” This article covers what is inside Ancestral Grain, which cats and dogs it suits, who it does not suit, and where it parts company with Prime.

In short

  • Ancestral Grain is not grain-free, it is low-grain — spelt and oats replace maize, rice and modern wheat.
  • The manufacturer states that the grain proportion is kept limited and animal content stays high.
  • It comes as single-main-protein combinations such as chicken & pomegranate or lamb & blueberry.
  • In animals with sensitive digestion and a history of soft stool it can be a gentler option than grain-free formulas.
  • It is a daily food for healthy adult cats and dogs — not a therapeutic diet.
  • Grain allergy is rare; itching is usually caused by the protein source rather than the grain.

What Is N&D Ancestral Grain?

Farmina N&D has five ranges, and Ancestral Grain is the middle path among them. In our Farmina N&D pet food guide we compared all of them: Prime removes grain entirely, Pumpkin leans on pumpkin for digestion, Quinoa is reserved for functional needs, and Ocean is built on fish protein. Ancestral Grain asks a different question — instead of taking grain out, what if we change which grain is used and how much of it?

As in Prime, the formulas are built around a single main protein: chicken & pomegranate, lamb & blueberry, codfish & orange. They come as dry and wet food, in puppy/kitten and adult variants, and on the dog side with mini / medium & maxi kibble sizes. We listed every food group we keep at the clinic in our cat and dog food range article.

What Does “Ancestral Grain” Mean?

Spelt and oats, not maize and rice

Ancestral grain describes cereals used before industrial farming took over — ones that selective breeding has changed relatively little. Spelt is one of the ancestors of modern bread wheat; oats stand out for their fibre structure. The choice in this range is to take the starch that dry food needs to hold kibble shape from these two grains rather than from maize or rice.

It is worth not overstating the distinction: spelt is still a wheat and it contains gluten. “Ancestral grain” is not a magic label that makes grain harmless; it only tells you which grain was chosen.

Why low glycaemic index comes up

Glycaemic index describes how quickly a carbohydrate raises blood sugar. Low-glycaemic sources release it more slowly, which softens the peaks and troughs after a meal and extends the feeling of fullness. In animals prone to weight gain, and when you are trying to hold a portion, that is a detail which helps in practice.

We are not saying this has a therapeutic effect. In a cat or dog diagnosed with diabetes, a daily food is not enough; that picture calls for a veterinary diet and regular monitoring.

Which Animals Does Ancestral Grain Suit?

  • Cats and dogs with sensitive digestion. In animals with a history of soft stool and gas on high-protein grain-free formulas, a low-grain formula is usually a gentler starting point.
  • Healthy adults. For cats and dogs without chronic illness and at a sensible weight, it is a reasonable daily food.
  • Puppies and kittens — with the growth formula. Energy and mineral balance differ during growth; an adult formula is not given to a growing animal.
  • Owners unsure whether grain-free is necessary. Dogs can use plant carbohydrate; in a healthy dog, grain-free feeding is a preference rather than a requirement.

Who Does It Not Suit?

  • Animals with a confirmed grain or gluten sensitivity. It is a rare picture, but if an elimination diet has demonstrated it, a formula containing grain is not the choice; the grain-free Prime range is more appropriate here.
  • Animals that need to lose weight. A low glycaemic index does not slim an animal on its own. If there is a weight target, you need a calorie-reduced formula and a written portion plan; for life after neutering, see our article on food for neutered dogs.
  • Animals with chronic disease. In kidney, liver or pancreatic disease, a history of urinary stones or diagnosed obesity, a daily food is not enough; a veterinary diet is required.
  • Underweight animals and those off their food. The cause needs to be found before the food is changed; weight loss on its own is not a food problem.

Prime or Ancestral Grain?

Both are balanced options, and the choice between them answers “which one fits this animal” rather than “which one is better.” In practice we separate them like this: in an active animal with untroubled stool and good tolerance for high protein, Prime goes down easily. In an animal whose digestion is upset by new foods, that has a history of soft stool, or whose portion you struggle to control, Ancestral Grain holds fewer surprises.

The decision comes from the animal rather than the label: age, breed, body condition score, current and target weight, stool history and any medical background.

Portions and the Food Transition

No food prevents weight gain when it is served without measuring. The table on the packet is a starting point; the amount is read against the animal's target weight, not today's weight. Use kitchen scales rather than a cup, split the daily amount into two meals, and keep treats under 10% of daily calories.

Change the food gradually over 7-10 days: a quarter new food for the first three days, half for the next three, then three quarters, and the full new food on day ten. Watch stool consistency and appetite throughout. If diarrhoea lasts longer than 48 hours, contains blood, or comes with loss of appetite, stop the transition and get in touch with us.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is N&D Ancestral Grain grain-free?

No, it is low-grain, as the name suggests. The formula contains a limited proportion of low-glycaemic ancestral grains such as spelt and oats. If you are looking for grain-free feeding, the range you want is Prime; Ancestral Grain does not aim to remove grain altogether but to use the right grain in a small amount.

What is the difference between Ancestral Grain and Prime?

Prime is grain-free and takes its carbohydrate from pea starch. Ancestral Grain contains a small amount of low-glycaemic ancestral grains such as spelt and oats. Both are balanced options; which one suits your pet depends on digestive sensitivity, weight and medical history.

Will the grain content cause an allergy in my dog?

Grain allergy is relatively rare in cats and dogs; most food allergies come from protein sources such as chicken, beef or dairy protein. If there is itching, redness or recurrent ear infection, an examination to find the cause is a shorter route than changing the food yourself.

Can Ancestral Grain be given to puppies and kittens?

Yes, but with the puppy and kitten formulas rather than the adult one. Energy density and the calcium-phosphorus balance differ during growth, and an adult formula falls short in a growing animal. In dogs you also need the variant that matches the breed size.

Can I feed Ancestral Grain to a neutered cat or dog?

You can, but the portion has to be kept tight. Daily energy needs fall after neutering and standard adult formulas do not account for that drop. In an animal that has started to gain weight, a calorie-reduced formula makes the job easier.

Do I need a transition period when moving from Prime to Ancestral Grain?

Yes. Even within the same brand the carbohydrate source changes, so spread the transition over 7-10 days and watch stool consistency throughout. An abrupt switch can cause soft stool and gas in animals with sensitive digestion.
