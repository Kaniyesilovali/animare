What Is N&D Pumpkin? The Grain-Free Range With Pumpkin

15 September 2026 · Dr. Gökay Yeşilovalı, Veterinary Surgeon

N&D Pumpkin is the range in which Farmina adds pumpkin to its grain-free formula: protein still comes mainly from meat and fish, there is no grain, and what makes the difference is the soluble fibre pumpkin brings. At the clinic, owners nearly always ask about this range in the same sentence: “My dog’s stool never quite settles — would switching to the pumpkin food fix it?” This article covers what is in N&D Pumpkin, what pumpkin actually does, which cats and dogs it suits, who it does not suit, and where it parts ways with Prime and Ancestral Grain.

In short

  • Pumpkin is grain-free — essentially Prime with pumpkin added.
  • Pumpkin supplies soluble fibre, largely pectin: it firms up the stool and steadies gut transit.
  • It comes as single-main-protein combinations such as chicken & pomegranate or lamb & blueberry.
  • It is considered for cats and dogs with sensitive stomachs whose stool consistency varies.
  • There is also a variant for neutered cats.
  • It is an everyday food — not a treatment for ongoing diarrhoea, which needs the cause found by examination.

What Is N&D Pumpkin?

Of the Farmina N&D ranges, Pumpkin sits closest to the heading “digestion”. Our Farmina N&D pet food guide compares all of them: Prime removes grain, Ancestral Grain uses a small amount of ancestral grain, Quinoa is set aside for functional needs and Ocean is built on fish protein. Pumpkin keeps the grain-free skeleton and adds a single variable on top — pumpkin.

As in the other ranges, the formulas are built around one main protein: chicken & pomegranate, lamb & blueberry and so on. They come as dry and wet food, in puppy/kitten and adult variants, and on the dog side split by kibble size into mini / medium & maxi; on the cat side there is a separate variant for neutered animals. We list every food group we keep at the clinic in our article on cat and dog food types.

What Does Pumpkin Actually Do?

Soluble fibre and stool consistency

What stands out about pumpkin is its soluble fibre, largely pectin. Soluble fibre holds water in the gut and forms a gel-like consistency, and that works in both directions. Where the stool is too soft it binds water and firms it up; where transit is too slow it adds bulk and eases things along. The picture we see in practice is a familiar one: in dogs that pass a normal stool in the morning and a soft one in the evening, this kind of fibre profile often settles the matter.

The second effect of soluble fibre is that bacteria in the large intestine ferment it. That fermentation produces short-chain fatty acids, which help feed the cells lining the gut. This is what is meant by a prebiotic effect, and it shows that fibre in food is not simply roughage.

Low in calories, filling

Pumpkin carries few calories for its bulk. That is a small but useful detail for owners wrestling with portion control: a fuller feeling for the same weight of food makes a difference in animals that hover by the bowl between meals. Even so, we would not call this a weight-loss effect; if there is a weight target, what you need is a calorie-reduced formula and a written portion plan.

Which Animals Does Pumpkin Suit?

  • Cats and dogs with variable stool consistency. In animals whose health screen came back clean but whose stool changes from day to day, it is one of the first options to try.
  • Animals that find high-protein grain-free formulas heavy. If there is a history of gas and soft stool on Prime, changing the fibre profile within the same grain-free frame is a sensible step.
  • Neutered cats. The variant set aside for this group accounts for the drop in energy needs.
  • Cats with hairball trouble. A higher fibre intake can help swallowed hair pass through the digestive tract.

Who Does It Not Suit?

  • Animals with ongoing diarrhoea. Changing food does not stand in for a diagnosis. Switching ranges before parasites, infection or a food intolerance have been ruled out only costs time.
  • Animals with chronic disease. In kidney, liver or pancreatic disease, or diagnosed obesity, an everyday food is not enough; a veterinary diet is needed.
  • Owners who actually want grain in the bowl. If you are after a low-grain formula, the right place is the Ancestral Grain range.
  • Animals that need to lose weight. Fibre helps with satiety but does not slim an animal on its own; for weight after neutering, see our article on food for neutered dogs.

Pumpkin, Prime or Ancestral Grain?

All three are balanced options; the choice between them is not “which is better” but “which variable are we moving in this animal”. In an active animal with untroubled digestion, the grain-free Prime range is already enough. If stool consistency varies and you want to stay grain-free, Pumpkin changes the fibre profile within the same frame. If grain-free is not a requirement and you are after a low-glycaemic carbohydrate source, Ancestral Grain fits better.

The decision comes from the animal rather than the label: age, breed, body condition score, current and target weight, the stool history of recent months and any medical history.

Portions and the Food Transition

The table on the packaging is a starting point; the amount is read against the animal's target ideal weight, not today's weight. Use kitchen scales rather than a cup, split the daily amount into two meals and keep treats under 10% of daily calories.

Change the food gradually over 7-10 days: a quarter new food for the first three days, half for the next three, then three quarters, and entirely the new food on day ten. Because the fibre content changes, you may see an increase in stool volume in the first few days; that is expected. If diarrhoea lasts more than 48 hours, contains blood or comes with loss of appetite, stop the transition and get in touch with us.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is N&D Pumpkin grain-free?

Yes. Pumpkin is a grain-free formula with pumpkin added; pea starch is used in place of grain. If you are looking for a low-grain food, the range you want is Ancestral Grain. What separates Pumpkin from Prime is not the grain but the soluble fibre the added pumpkin brings.

Does pumpkin really help digestion?

Pumpkin contains soluble fibre, largely pectin, which holds water in the gut, firms up the stool and steadies transit time. In animals with a history of mildly soft stool this can make a practical difference. But pumpkin is not a medicine: if parasites, infection or a food intolerance are behind the diarrhoea, food alone will not solve it.

My dog has diarrhoea. Is switching to Pumpkin enough?

No. Ongoing diarrhoea needs the cause found first — a faecal examination, a parasite screen and, if needed, blood tests. If the diarrhoea lasts more than 48 hours, contains blood, or comes with vomiting or loss of appetite, stop changing foods and bring your pet in. Pumpkin is an everyday food for animals with sensitive digestion, not a treatment for a diagnosed illness.

What is the difference between Pumpkin and Prime?

Both are grain-free with a high proportion of animal protein. Pumpkin additionally contains pumpkin, which changes the fibre profile and makes it a steadier option for animals whose stool consistency varies. In an active animal with untroubled digestion, Prime is already enough.

Can I feed Pumpkin to a neutered cat?

Yes, the range includes a variant prepared for neutered cats. Daily energy needs fall after neutering; at that stage, setting the portion against the cat's ideal weight matters more than which range you pick.

Could I just add pumpkin to the food myself?

Plain boiled pumpkin is not harmful to cats and dogs, but once the amount gets away from you it upsets the daily calorie balance and dilutes the calculated nutrient ratios of the food. If you are thinking of adding it regularly, agree the amount with your vet, and do not use spiced, sweetened or pie-filling pumpkin products.
