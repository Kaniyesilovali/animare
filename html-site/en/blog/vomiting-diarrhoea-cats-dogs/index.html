Vomiting and Diarrhoea in Cats and Dogs: When Is It an Emergency?

26 October 2026 · Dr. Gökay Yeşilovalı, Veterinarian

Did your dog vomit once and then finish its breakfast, or has it been bringing up everything since the morning? Vomiting and diarrhoea in cats and dogs is usually a simple stomach upset that settles within a day. But the same signs can also be the first warning of poisoning, a bowel obstruction or a serious infection such as parvovirus. This article separates what you can watch at home from the signs that mean you should see a vet without waiting.

In short

  • A single episode of vomiting or soft stool can usually be watched at home if your pet is bright and eating.
  • Repeated vomiting, blood in vomit or stool, weakness and a swollen belly are emergency signs.
  • Vomiting and bloody diarrhoea in an unvaccinated puppy must be checked for parvovirus straight away.
  • A cat that eats nothing for more than a day has a serious problem in its own right.
  • Never give human medicines without asking your vet.

What Causes Vomiting and Diarrhoea?

Vomiting and diarrhoea are signs, not diseases. The most common causes in cats and dogs are:

  • Eating something from the bin, the street or the table
  • A sudden change of food
  • Intestinal parasites
  • Viral and bacterial infections
  • Swallowing a toxic food, plant or household chemical
  • Foreign bodies such as toys, bones or string
  • Kidney, liver or pancreatic disease

Cats often bring up the occasional hairball. If it happens several times a week, though, do not treat it as normal; book an examination.

Vomiting or regurgitation?

When a pet vomits, it first becomes restless, drools and brings food up with strong abdominal effort. Regurgitation is effortless: undigested food comes back up, often straight after eating. The difference helps separate problems of the oesophagus from problems of the stomach and intestines. Telling your vet which one you saw speeds up the diagnosis.

When Are Vomiting and Diarrhoea an Emergency?

If any of the following applies, do not wait: see a vet the same day.

Emergency signs with vomiting

  • Vomiting again and again within a few hours, unable to keep even water down
  • Blood in the vomit, or vomit that looks like coffee grounds
  • A dog retching without bringing anything up while its belly swells; this can mean a twisted stomach, and minutes count
  • Suspected poisoning or a swallowed foreign body
  • A male cat that vomits and cannot pass urine (see our article on urinary blockage in cats)

Emergency signs with diarrhoea

  • Stool with a lot of blood, or black, tar-like stool
  • Watery diarrhoea lasting more than a day
  • Diarrhoea together with vomiting, fever or abdominal pain

Pets at higher risk

Puppies, kittens, older pets and those with a chronic condition such as kidney disease or diabetes cope much worse with fluid loss, so the threshold for them is lower. If an unvaccinated puppy has vomiting and bloody diarrhoea, parvovirus has to be considered; we explain the vaccination programme in our dog vaccination schedule. In cats, loss of appetite for more than a day needs a vet check even without vomiting.

Weakness, being unable to stand, pale gums or collapse are always an emergency. For a fuller list, read our article on pet emergency signs, or contact our emergency service directly if needed.

When Can You Watch at Home?

If your pet vomited once or passed one soft stool and is bright, playful and drinking afterwards, you can watch for a few hours. During that time:

  • Keep fresh water available at all times.
  • Note how often your pet vomits or passes stool, the colour and the time; take photos if you can.
  • Block access to bins, table scraps and garden plants.
  • If signs do not settle, or any of the signs above appear, call straight away.

What not to do at home

  • Do not give anti-diarrhoeal, stomach or pain medicines sold for people; some are toxic to cats and dogs.
  • Do not force your pet to eat or drink.
  • Do not try to make your pet vomit yourself. If poisoning is suspected, the vet decides what to do; our article on toxic foods and plants lists the dangerous ones.

What Do We Check at the Clinic?

The examination starts with how dehydrated your pet is, whether the abdomen is painful and whether there is a fever. Depending on the likely cause, we may then need:

  • Blood tests: show dehydration, kidney and liver values and signs of infection. They are run in our clinic laboratory; our blood test guide explains what the values mean.
  • Stool examination: reveals intestinal parasites and some infections.
  • X-ray: a radiology examination when a foreign body or obstruction is suspected.

Treatment depends on the cause: fluid support, anti-nausea medicine, parasite treatment, a change of diet or, if there is a foreign body, surgery. When problems last for weeks or keep coming back, the food is reviewed as well; our nutrition consulting service can help.

Frequently Asked Questions

My dog vomited once. Should I go to the vet?

If your dog is bright, drinking and does not vomit again, you can watch at home for a few hours. If the vomiting comes back, you see blood in it, the belly swells or your dog becomes weak, see a vet the same day.

Is it normal for my cat to vomit?

An occasional hairball is common in cats. Vomiting several times a week, weight loss or poor appetite is not normal, though, and needs an examination for kidney disease, intestinal parasites and digestive problems.

How long can diarrhoea last before my dog needs a vet?

See a vet if watery diarrhoea lasts more than a day. If there is a lot of blood in the stool, vomiting as well, or your dog is a puppy or elderly, do not wait: have it examined the same day.

Can I give human medicine to a pet that is vomiting?

No. Some anti-diarrhoeal, stomach and pain medicines sold for people poison cats and dogs. The right medicine and dose are chosen by a vet once the cause is known.

Why are vomiting and bloody diarrhoea dangerous in a puppy?

Puppies are affected by fluid loss very quickly. In a puppy whose vaccinations are not complete, vomiting and bloody diarrhoea can be a sign of parvovirus infection. Early treatment is vital with this disease, so see a vet without waiting.
