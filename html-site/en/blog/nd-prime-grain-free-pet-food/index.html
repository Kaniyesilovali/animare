What Is N&D Prime? The Grain-Free, High-Animal-Content Core Range

6 September 2026 · Dr. Gökay Yeşilovalı, Veterinary Surgeon

N&D Prime is Farmina's grain-free, high-animal-content core range: there is no grain in the formula, the protein comes mainly from meat and fish, and pea starch is used as the carbohydrate source. The question we hear most often at the clinic is this: “Is grain-free food better?” The short answer is, not for every animal. This article covers what is inside Prime, which cats and dogs it suits, and who it does not suit.

In short

  • N&D Prime is grain-free; pea starch replaces grain.
  • The manufacturer states that the range keeps animal content high and balances the rest with fruit and vegetables.
  • It comes as single-main-protein combinations such as chicken & pomegranate or lamb & blueberry.
  • It is an everyday food for healthy, active adult cats and dogs — not a therapeutic diet.
  • Grain allergy is rare; most food allergies are protein-driven, so “grain-free” alone is not an allergy solution.
  • Kidney, liver or pancreatic disease and diagnosed obesity call for a veterinary diet, not an everyday range.

What Is N&D Prime?

Farmina N&D has five ranges, and Prime is arguably the starting point of the family. Our Farmina N&D pet food guide compares all of them: Ancestral Grain includes ancestral grains, Pumpkin leans on pumpkin for digestion, Quinoa covers functional needs, and Ocean is built on fish protein. Prime follows a simple logic — remove the grain, keep the animal content high, balance the rest with fruit and vegetables.

Formulas are built around a single main protein: chicken & pomegranate, lamb & blueberry, boar & apple. The range comes as dry and wet food, in puppy/kitten and adult variants, and on the dog side it is split by kibble size into mini and medium & maxi. Which variants are in stock changes from period to period; we list every food group we keep at the clinic in our cat and dog food range article.

What Do “Grain-Free” and “High Animal Content” Actually Mean?

What grain-free means, and what it does not

Grain-free means the formula contains no wheat, corn, rice or barley. It does not mean carbohydrate has been removed altogether — dry food needs starch to hold its kibble shape, and Prime takes that from peas.

The common misunderstanding runs like this: “My dog is itching, let's switch to grain-free.” Grain allergy is relatively rare in cats and dogs; most food allergies come from chicken, beef or dairy protein. If switching to a grain-free food means moving from one chicken formula to another chicken formula, the skin signs will not change. Where a genuine food allergy is suspected, the right route is an elimination diet run under veterinary supervision.

The animal content ratio

What sets Prime apart is that the protein requirement is met mainly from animal sources. This matters most for cats: the cat is an obligate carnivore and cannot get enough of certain nutrients, such as taurine and arachidonic acid, from plant sources. The picture is more flexible in dogs — a dog can use plant carbohydrate, which is why grain-free feeding is a preference rather than a requirement on the dog side.

The analytical values on the bag vary by variant; crude protein is noticeably higher in the cat formulas than in the dog ones. Formulas are updated from time to time, so it is worth reading the values on the pack you actually buy.

Which Animals Does Prime Suit?

  • Healthy, active adult dogs. For dogs with no weight problem or chronic illness and a reasonable amount of daily movement, it is a sensible everyday food.
  • Adult cats. The high animal protein ratio sits close to the cat's natural feeding pattern.
  • Puppies and kittens — with the growth formula. The calcium-phosphorus balance and energy density differ during growth; an adult formula is not given to a growing animal.
  • Pets fed wet food alongside dry. In cats that drink little water in particular, wet food support contributes to urinary tract health.

Who Is Prime Not Right For?

To be honest about it, this is not the right choice for every animal:

  • Neutered pets and those prone to weight gain. Daily energy needs drop after neutering, and a reduced-calorie formula makes the job easier. For detail, see our article on food for neutered dogs.
  • Pets with sensitive digestion. Where there is a history of soft stool and gas, Pumpkin or Ancestral Grain can be a gentler starting point.
  • Pets with chronic illness. In kidney, liver or pancreatic disease, a history of urinary stones or diagnosed obesity, an everyday food is not enough; a veterinary diet is required.
  • Dogs with a history of heart disease. The debate around grain-free diets and dilated cardiomyopathy centres on legume-heavy formulas in particular. If your dog has a cardiac history, discuss the food choice with your vet.

Portions and the Food Transition

No food prevents weight gain when it is served without measuring. The table on the bag is a starting point; the amount is read against the animal's target weight, not today's weight. Use a kitchen scale rather than a cup, split the daily amount into two meals, and keep treats under 10% of daily calories.

Change the food gradually over 7-10 days: a quarter new food for the first three days, half and half for the next three, three quarters after that, and the full amount on day ten. Watch stool consistency and appetite throughout. If diarrhoea lasts longer than 48 hours, contains blood, or comes with loss of appetite, stop the transition and get in touch with us.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between N&D Prime and Ancestral Grain?

Prime is grain-free and takes its carbohydrate from pea starch. Ancestral Grain contains a small amount of low-glycaemic ancestral grains such as spelt and oats. Both are balanced options; which one suits your pet depends on digestive sensitivity, weight and medical history.

Does my dog need grain-free food?

No. Dogs can use plant carbohydrate, and grain allergy is rare. For a healthy dog, grain-free feeding is a preference rather than a requirement. If there is itching or a digestive complaint, an examination to find the cause is a shorter route than changing the food yourself.

Can N&D Prime be given to puppies and kittens?

Yes, but with the puppy and kitten formulas rather than the adult one. Energy, protein and the calcium-phosphorus balance differ during growth, and an adult formula falls short in a growing animal.

Does grain-free Prime carry a heart disease risk?

The link between grain-free diets and dilated cardiomyopathy is still debated and no clear cause-and-effect relationship has been established; the discussion centres mainly on legume-heavy formulas. There is nothing here that should worry the owner of a healthy pet, but if your dog has a history of heart disease or belongs to a predisposed breed, choose the food together with your vet.

Can I feed the same Prime food to my cat and my dog?

No. Cat and dog formulas differ in protein level, fatty acid profile and cat-specific nutrients such as taurine. A cat fed on dog food can develop deficiencies over time.

Do I need a transition period when moving from Prime to another range?

Yes. Even within the same brand the ingredients change, so spread the transition over 7-10 days. If you are only changing the protein source within the same range (for example chicken to lamb) you can shorten it slightly, but avoid an abrupt switch.
