Cat and Dog Food Range: Which Formula Suits Which Pet?

14 August 2026 · Animare Veterinary

Standing in front of a shelf of pet food and wondering which bag belongs to your cat is a very common experience. The same brand offers ten different formulas — one with pumpkin, one with pomegranate, one with spirulina. In this guide we go through the food lines we keep at our clinic and explain which pet each one is for, and why. The point is not to walk you round the shelf, but to make the right question easier to ask.

Short answer: we keep three main groups of food

  • Everyday diets — for healthy puppies, kittens, adults and neutered pets (Farmina N&D lines, Matisse, Cibau, Ecopet, Fun).
  • Veterinary diets — for managing a diagnosed condition, used under veterinary supervision (Farmina Vet Life).
  • Wet food and treats — cans that increase water intake, recovery formulas, and dental treats.

Everyday Diets: The N&D Lines and Who They Suit

Farmina N&D (Natural & Delicious) lines are made for the everyday feeding of healthy cats and dogs. What separates them is the protein source, the approach to grain, and the functional ingredients added:

  • N&D Prime (grain-free): The core line, with chicken & pomegranate and lamb & blueberry formulas built around a high animal-ingredient content. The usual starting point for healthy, active pets. Kitten, adult and neutered options for cats; puppy and adult, mini and medium/maxi options for dogs.
  • N&D Ancestral Grain (low grain): Contains small amounts of low-glycaemic ancestral grains such as spelt and oats. A balanced option for pets that do not need to avoid grain, in chicken & pomegranate and lamb & blueberry versions.
  • N&D Pumpkin: A grain-free formula with pumpkin added to support digestion. Worth considering for pets whose stool consistency fluctuates. A neutered version is available for cats.
  • N&D Tropical: Lamb protein combined with tropical fruits and vegetables. Puppy and adult, mini and medium/maxi for dogs; chicken, lamb and neutered options for cats.
  • N&D Ocean: Built on fish proteins — cod, salmon, herring and shrimp. One of the first alternatives we try when chicken sensitivity is suspected. Kitten and puppy formulas are also available.
  • N&D Spirulina: Spirulina-enriched formulas, with lamb for dogs and herring for cats.
  • N&D Quinoa (functional): Targeted formulas for a specific need. We keep Skin & Coat (herring) and, for cats, Hairball.

Most lines come in a smaller kibble for mini breeds and a larger one for medium/maxi breeds, in both small and large bags — a large bag makes sense in a multi-pet household, a small one while you are still trialling a food.

Coat-Specific Formulas: White Dog and Brown Dog

In light-coated mini breeds such as Maltese, Bichon and Pomeranian, staining around the eyes and mouth is one of the complaints we hear most. N&D White Dog is formulated for light coats with sea bass, potato, spirulina and fennel; N&D Brown Dog uses lamb, potato, spirulina and carrot for darker coats. Both come in puppy and adult mini versions.

One honest caveat: if the staining is driven by a blocked tear duct, an inturned eyelash or an allergic skin condition, no food will resolve it on its own. We recommend having the eyes and skin examined before switching to this line.

Puppy, Adult, Neutered: Why the Variant Matters

  • Puppies and kittens: Growth changes the requirement for energy, protein, calcium and phosphorus. Feeding adult food during growth can hold development back.
  • Adults: A standard formula matched to activity level and breed size is enough.
  • Neutered pets: Energy needs fall after neutering while appetite often rises. Lower-calorie "neutered" formulas make weight control easier — but no food prevents weight gain if portions are never measured.
  • Senior pets: Joints, kidneys and weight take priority. In this age group the food is best chosen alongside blood and urine test results.

Veterinary Diets: The Vet Life Range

Vet Life is a range of therapeutic diets formulated to support the management of specific conditions. These are not everyday foods for healthy pets; they are part of a treatment plan for a diagnosed problem, which is why they are dispensed with veterinary assessment and follow-up.

Struvite and Struvite Management make the point well: the first is used short-term to dissolve stones, the second long-term to stop them coming back. When even two similarly named foods differ in how long they should be fed, "my friend's cat is on this one" is not a good guide.

Wet Food and Cans

Cats naturally drink little, and increasing water intake matters more than most owners expect for urinary and kidney health. Wet food is the easiest way to do it. What we keep:

  • N&D cat cans: Ocean (tuna, cod, shrimp, pumpkin) in kitten and adult, Prime (chicken & pomegranate) in kitten and adult, and lamb, pumpkin & blueberry.
  • N&D dog cans: Ocean (salmon & codfish), Prime (chicken & pomegranate), and lamb, pumpkin & blueberry.
  • Matisse Cat Mousse Salmon: A mousse texture that is readily accepted by cats with oral discomfort or a poor appetite.
  • Vet Life Natural Diet cans: Convalescence, Gastrointestinal and UltraHypo for dogs; Convalescence, Gastrointestinal and Renal for cats. These make a real difference in patients that refuse dry food after surgery or during illness.

Dry food does not have to go entirely. Converting part of the daily portion to wet food is enough for most pets — as long as the total calorie count is still accounted for.

Dental Treats: Rewarding Without Ignoring the Teeth

Tartar is among the most common and most overlooked problems we see. N&D Dental Treats extend chewing time and help reduce plaque build-up, with White Dog and Brown Dog options for mini breeds and chicken & pomegranate and lamb & blueberry for medium/maxi breeds. They support tooth brushing rather than replacing it — and they belong in the daily calorie count.

Other Lines: Matisse, Cibau, Ecopet and Fun

  • Matisse (cats): An everyday line with kitten, chicken & rice, salmon & tuna, neutered and salmon neutered formulas.
  • Cibau (dogs): Adult Medium alongside Sensitive Lamb and Sensitive Fish medium/maxi formulas for sensitive digestion.
  • Ecopet Natural (dogs): Adult Medium and lamb medium formulas — a straightforward, economical everyday option.
  • Fun (dogs and cats): Adult and Energy for dogs, fish and meat for cats, in large bags — practical for multi-pet households and shelter feeding.

The Four Questions We Ask Before Choosing a Food

When you come in and ask which food to buy, we work through these in order:

  • How old is the pet, and is it neutered? This decides the variant — puppy/kitten, adult or neutered.
  • What is the body condition? If there is excess weight, calorie density and a portion plan come first.
  • Are there digestive or skin complaints? Recurrent diarrhoea, itching or ear infections may call for a different protein source or a hypoallergenic trial.
  • Is there a diagnosed condition? With kidney, liver, diabetic, urinary or joint disease, we are no longer talking about everyday food but about a veterinary diet.

Once those four are answered, the shortlist usually comes down to two or three options. The rest is a matter of taste preference and what the stool consistency tells us.

Switching Food: The 7–10 Day Rule

However good a food is, an abrupt change can cause vomiting and diarrhoea. Introduce the new food gradually: 25% for the first three days, 50% for the next three, then 75%, and the full new food on day ten. Watch stool consistency throughout; if diarrhoea lasts longer than 24–48 hours, call +90 533 844 91 92. For more on the N&D lines and food transitions, see our Farmina N&D pet food guide.

Nutrition Advice in Nicosia and Gönyeli

At our clinic in Gönyeli / Nicosia we do not treat food choice as separate from the examination: we weigh your pet, assess body condition score, review blood and urine results where relevant, and choose the line together with you. We write the daily portion down in grams and book a short weight check a few weeks later. For patients on a veterinary diet that follow-up matters even more — whether a food is working can only be judged by measuring.

If you are unsure which food suits your cat or dog, get in touch before you change the bag.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between food bought at a vet clinic and food from a supermarket?

The difference lies less in the brand than in how the choice is made. At the clinic the food is selected after considering your pet's age, weight, neuter status, digestive history and any diagnosed disease, and a veterinary diet is used only when it is genuinely needed. Just as importantly, we check whether the food is working through weight monitoring and follow-up visits.

Can Vet Life food be given to any pet?

No. Vet Life is a range of therapeutic diets formulated for specific conditions: kidney disease, liver disease, diabetes, urinary stones, joint problems, obesity, sensitive digestion and food allergy. Their nutrient balance is deliberately adjusted for the target condition, so they are not suitable for long-term use in a healthy pet. They are dispensed after examination and diagnosis, with a defined duration of use.

Which food suits my neutered cat?

After neutering, energy needs drop while appetite often rises, so weight gain is common. Formulas made for neutered pets are the sensible choice; we keep neutered variants of the N&D Prime, Tropical, Pumpkin, Ocean and Ancestral Grain lines. Measuring the daily portion against your cat's ideal weight matters just as much as the food you pick.

Does my white-coated dog need White Dog food?

It is not essential, though tear and coat staining around the eyes and mouth is a common complaint in light-coated dogs. N&D White Dog is made for light-coated mini breeds with sea bass, potato, spirulina and fennel, while Brown Dog uses lamb, potato, spirulina and carrot for darker coats. If the staining is caused by a blocked tear duct or an allergy, food alone will not fix it — an examination comes first.

Should my cat eat wet food?

Wet food increases water intake, which helps urinary and kidney health, and it also helps pets that are off their food, recovering from illness or elderly. You do not have to drop dry food entirely; converting part of the daily portion to wet food is enough for most pets. We keep N&D cat and dog cans, Matisse mousse and Vet Life Natural Diet cans at the clinic.

How should I switch my pet to a new food?

Spread the change over 7 to 10 days: a quarter new food for the first three days, half for the next three, three quarters after that, and the full new food on day ten. Watch stool consistency and appetite throughout. If diarrhoea lasts longer than 24–48 hours, contains blood, or comes with loss of appetite, call the clinic.
