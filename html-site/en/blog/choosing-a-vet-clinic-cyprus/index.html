How to Choose a Vet Clinic in Cyprus

23 August 2026 · Dr. Gökay Yeşilovalı, Veterinarian

Your pet is part of the family, and choosing the right vet clinic in Cyprus is one of the decisions you will live with the longest. There is no shortage of options on the island, but clinics differ in equipment, in-house diagnostics and how reachable they are once the doors close for the day. This guide sets out the concrete questions worth asking before you decide.

In short

  • In-house laboratory and X-ray turn a diagnosis around in hours rather than days.
  • Find out how out-of-hours emergencies are handled before you have one, and save the number.
  • Leishmaniasis and tick-borne disease are a real island risk — a clinic that asks about parasite prevention is a good sign.
  • Being able to talk in the language you are comfortable with is half of getting treatment right.
  • Continuity with the same vet is worth more than shopping around each time.

Why the Choice Matters More Than It Seems

A veterinary clinic is not only somewhere you go when something is wrong. Regular general check-ups, vaccines and preventive care are the foundation of a long, healthy life. The right clinic is there both for the routine and for the day nothing goes to plan.

Continuity itself has medical value. A vet who has seen your animal for years knows its weight curve, its bloodwork history and its temperament, which is exactly what makes a small deviation stand out early. Starting again at a different clinic each time resets that record. A good vet clinic in Cyprus shows it in a team you trust, clear explanations and notes that are properly kept.

What a Well-Equipped Clinic Should Offer

Reading up on the services before you visit saves time. In a full-service clinic you should expect to find:

Routine Care and Vaccination

  • Regular health checks and general examination
  • A tracked vaccination plan including rabies and combination vaccines — our dog vaccination schedule and kitten vaccination schedule show how the timing works
  • Internal and external parasite protection (fleas, ticks, worms)
  • Microchipping and identification

Diagnostics and Treatment

The question to ask is simple: are tests and imaging done here, or sent away? A clinic with its own laboratory and radiology gives you bloods, X-ray and ultrasound results within the same visit. When you are looking for a foreign body in a vomiting dog or checking glucose in a collapsed cat, those hours are the difference.

Surgery and Dental Care

Surgical procedures, neutering and dental care require an experienced team, a properly sterile setup and uninterrupted monitoring under anaesthesia. Do ask whether pre-operative bloodwork is run and how the patient is monitored during the procedure — a good clinic is glad to answer. Our guide to dental care in cats explains when tartar and gum disease actually need intervention.

The Cyprus Factor: Parasite Pressure on the Island

The Mediterranean climate gives pets here a different disease profile from the mainland. Whether a clinic knows that profile well is the most commonly overlooked criterion of all.

  • Leishmaniasis. Transmitted to dogs by the sandfly, it can surface months later and needs lifelong monitoring. See our guide to Leishmaniasis in dogs.
  • Tick-borne disease. Agents such as Ehrlichia and Babesia are common here and diagnosis rests on bloodwork — the signs are listed in tick-borne diseases in dogs.
  • Year-round ectoparasites. Flea and tick pressure never fully stops in winter, so protection has to run all twelve months — covered in ectoparasite protection.
  • Heatstroke. One of the most frequent summer emergencies; prevention is set out in summer heat and pet safety.

A practical test: if the vet asks at the first consultation which parasite product you use and how often, you are in the right place.

Emergencies: Why Out-of-Hours Cover Is Critical

Accidents and sudden illness do not wait for opening time, so find out how the clinic handles emergencies in advance: is there a number to call at night, who answers it, and can your pet stay in overnight?

Knowing which signs genuinely count as an emergency saves time too; our guide to pet emergency signs lists them in full. The following mean leaving straight away:

  • Difficulty breathing or a blue-tinged tongue
  • Vomiting or diarrhoea that will not stop, or blood in the stool
  • Suspected poisoning
  • Serious injury or bleeding that will not stop
  • A male cat unable to pass urine
  • Seizures, collapse or altered consciousness

Saving the clinic's contact details to your phone today is the single most useful preparation you can make.

Language and Communication

North Cyprus has a large community of foreign residents with pets. Misreading a treatment plan, a dose or a follow-up date matters as much as the treatment itself, so make sure the clinic can work in the language you are comfortable with. At Animare we consult in both Turkish and English; a Turkish version of this guide is also available.

Five Steps to the Right Clinic

  1. Get to know the team. Look at what the vets specialise in; it matters the day surgery is on the table. Our team page is one example of what to look for.
  2. Ask about equipment. Are laboratory, X-ray and ultrasound on site, and how quickly do results come back?
  3. Work out the journey. Know how long it takes you to get there in an emergency. We are in Gönyeli / Lefkoşa; judge your own clinic by the drive at the busiest time of day.
  4. Use the first visit as a trial. Start with a routine check-up and watch the hygiene, how your pet is handled and how your questions are answered.
  5. Read reviews, then verify in person. Other owners' experience is a useful signal; make the final call with your own eyes.

Prevention Beats Treatment

Regular checks catch most problems before they become serious. One general examination a year, a vaccination schedule that is kept to, twelve months of parasite protection and a balanced diet shape your pet's quality of life more than anything else. From seven years of age, adding annual bloodwork is the most practical way to catch kidney and liver problems before any sign shows.

In Closing

The right vet clinic in Cyprus is a long-term partner in your pet's health. Weigh up the services offered, the out-of-hours arrangement, the diagnostic capability on site and how the team communicates. And remember that the best care starts with prevention, before anything goes wrong.

Frequently Asked Questions

How often should I visit a vet clinic in Cyprus?

A healthy adult cat or dog should have a general check-up at least once a year. Puppies and kittens come in every few weeks during their vaccination course, while pets over seven and those with chronic conditions are better seen every six months. On the island there is one extra rule: monthly internal and external parasite protection should never be skipped.

What should I do in an emergency outside working hours?

Stay calm and move your pet safely, using a carrier or a blanket. Call the clinic emergency line before you set off so the team can prepare while you travel. If poisoning is suspected, bring the packaging or a piece of the plant involved. Animare provides 24/7 emergency care; our emergency number is +90 533 844 91 92.

What matters most when choosing a clinic?

Four things stand out: whether the clinic runs its own laboratory and X-ray, whether out-of-hours cover is clearly defined, the standard of hygiene and equipment, and communication. A good vet explains the options and the cost in plain terms and never leaves your questions unanswered. The right clinic is the one where you feel comfortable and can keep seeing the same vet over time.

Do clinics in North Cyprus offer service in English?

Many pet owners on the island are foreign residents, so this is an important criterion and it varies between clinics. To avoid misunderstanding a treatment plan, a drug dose or a follow-up date, make sure the clinic can communicate in the language you are comfortable with. At Animare we work in both Turkish and English.

Does living in Cyprus carry extra risks for my pet?

The Mediterranean climate brings certain diseases to the fore. Leishmaniasis, transmitted to dogs by the sandfly, and tick-borne diseases are a real risk here, and heatstroke is common in summer. That is why it matters that your clinic offers a year-round parasite prevention plan and has the laboratory capability to diagnose these conditions.
